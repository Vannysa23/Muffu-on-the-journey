Well, it's kind of embarrassing to say that I walked into this IT field without knowing what the IT is actually about. So, why did I choose this path?

In the last two months before high school diploma exit exam, I started to think seriously about what major i was going to pursuit in university. I had 3 options back then:
+ Follow my heart: go for a business major
+ Follow what other people think about my potential: go to IR 
+ Follow the economy and future benefit: IT 

Hmmm, I didn't have much time to even ask myself properly what I - myself - actually want to go next. I was left with two options: IR or IT. These two was completely different from each others. I started to do research about IT the last two months, and I decided to go with IT. Why?
I thought this IT skill would benefit me in the long-term. This skill would be needed in every place and it would never become an in the outdated skill in the future. The more modern technology becomes, the more this skill flourishes. I was not completely wrong, tho, just it's not as easy as I thought. 

I have a lots of thoughts about the university I went to. I was once hated it so much that I didn't even want to step into that university. I was once regretted choosing that university for my IT major, I was once disappointed in their academic system for my major, I was once wanted to change my path from IT to business as I thought IT was not for me. I was once regret saying "Environment doesn't matter, we can still grow and learn by ourself no matter where we are," before enrolling in that university. The university was not that bad, but I would say the IT department was. That's on me too, I didn't do deep research on the major and the university I chose.

But never mind, that is a lesson I have learned.

The most important skill you need to acquire in your university life before anything else is **Self-Learning**. GODDAMN - I was poor in self-learning. The progress of my self-learning was pretty slow, and I was lost and frustrated. In high school, I was taught by someone else. I got use to learning by being guided, by knowing exactly what to follow next - the steps I had to take. Then, when I got to university, I had to learn by myself most of the time. That was a huge challenge for me. I got lost a lot of times, and I didn't know who should I ask for advice. I was completely left by myself. When I realized the department I was in couldn't help me with anything, and if I solely depend on its curriculum, I would have nothing to apply for job. I started hating the university from then on.  I trusted its popular reputation, but that was a lie. 99% of the students who chose to study in that department was not there for knowledge, they aimed for the certificate only. That was the hardest truth I learned. I hated the culture of extra classes -​​ រៀនគួរ, so I would never spend my money on those classes again. Whether I wanted to or not, I had to learn by myself.

That got me into a stress loop. My progress was slow -> doubting myself -> getting lost -> cry -> wanting to change major -> not wanting to give up -> starting self-learning again -> the progress was slow -> doubting myself -> getting lost -> cry -> .....

I was many steps behind others. \
But gratefully, I didn't give up. \
Actually I hate giving up.

In the third year of university, I applied and successfully got selected for a full web development course sponsored by the Ministry of Post and Telecommunication. I was one of the first-generation students studying at ANT Technology Center. That was the time I had hope and confidence in my skills again. Little did I know that one opportunity would open many more opportunities for me and would be the one that took me further than I expected.

The lessons I learned from this journey:
+ **"Environment matters"** --- Put yourself where you think it can take you further. Put yourself where you think you can grow. Surround yourself with people who have the same goals as you or even greater ones. Don't underestimate the environment you are in. It can push you up or eventually bring you down. 
+ **"Don't easily give up"** --- If you have already put your foot into something, don't give up on it easily. Today you are struggling, but who knows—you might get offered something next week or even tomorrow. The future cannot be foreseen.
+ **"Don't wait until you feel ready"** --- If you wait until you feel ready to do something, you will never be ready. I read an article that said, **"Ready isn't a feeling, it is a decision."** Well, that's kind of true.
